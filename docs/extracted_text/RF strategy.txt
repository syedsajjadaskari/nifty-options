1  Strategy at a Glance 
The RF (Reverse Failure) Strategy is a price-action setup that is the opposite of the IRF strategy. 
It is traded strictly in a confirmed uptrend, and it uses the high and low of the first 5-minute 
candle as its reference range. 
In plain words: the market opens with a gap up, dips below the bottom of the opening 5-minute candle, 
closes below it, and then comes back inside that range. That specific sequence is what switches the RF 
setup on. The gap itself must also stay within yesterday's trading range; a huge gap is rejected.

Key terms 
TERM MEANING 
RF zone The horizontal band between the top of the first 5-minute candle's upper wick and the bottom of its lower wick. 
Gap up The market opens above where it previously closed, leaving an empty space on the chart. 
Close below A proper candle close under the RF Low. A wick poking below is not enough; the candle must finish there. 
Return inside After that close below, price moves back into the original RF zone. This completes the activation. 
Yesterday's range The high-to-low range of the previous trading day. A gap up must stay within it to be accepted. 
200 EMA A basic trend filter. Useful, but never the only proof of an uptrend. 
 
NOT A GENERAL BREAKOUT STRATEGY 
RF only works through one specific combination: established uptrend + gap-up open within yesterday's 
range + a close below the first candle's low + a return inside that range. Miss any piece and there is no 
RF trade.

2  Identifying the First 5-Minute Range 
Everything in RF is measured against one candle: the first 5-minute candle after the market opens. You 
mark its extremes with two horizontal lines. The area between them is the RF zone. 
 
Figure 1  –  The RF zone is built from the whole first candle, wicks included. 
1 
Find the first 5-minute candle 
Locate the first 5-minute candle printed after the market opens. 
 
2 
Mark the highest point of the upper wick 
Draw a horizontal line at that level. This is the RF High. 
 
3 
Mark the lowest point of the lower wick 
Draw a horizontal line at that level. This is the RF Low, the line price must later close below. 
 
IMPORTANT 
Consider the entire candle, including both wicks. Using only the candle body gives a smaller, incorrect 
zone.

3  RF Activation Conditions 
The RF setup activates only when this exact price sequence happens, in order. The chart shows a 
textbook example (illustrative candles, not real data). 
 
Figure 2  –  Uptrend, gap-up open, close below the RF Low, return inside the range. 
1 
Confirm the uptrend 
The market must already be in a proper uptrend. Trading above the 200 EMA is a basic filter for 
this. 
 
2 
Confirm a gap-up opening 
The market must open with a gap up, and the gap must stay within yesterday's trading range. A 
gap-down open, or a gap beyond yesterday's range, invalidates the setup. 
 
3 
Price breaks below the lower boundary 
After the first 5-minute range is marked, price must move below the lower wick and produce a 
proper candle close below the range. 
 
4 
Price returns inside the range 
Following that confirmed close below, price must come back inside the original 5-minute range. 
This completes the RF activation sequence.

4  Trend Filter: the 200 EMA 
RF must be used only in a well-established uptrend, not merely because the current price sits above the 
200 EMA. The 200 EMA is a basic filter. It must not be treated as the sole confirmation of an uptrend. 
 
Figure 3  –  Same indicator, very different context. Only the left chart qualifies.

Reason for rejection: a sudden gap above the 200 
EMA does not by itself establish an uptrend. The 
bullish trend must already be demonstrated.

A gap down automatically invalidates RF. No gap up, no RF, regardless of what the 5-minute candle 
later does.

6  Yesterday's Range Filter 
A huge gap is rejected. Only a gap up that stays within yesterday's trading range is accepted. If 
the gap carries the open beyond yesterday's trading range (above yesterday's high), the RF 
setup is not activated. 
 
Figure 5  –  Same gap-up idea, two outcomes: inside yesterday's range is accepted, beyond it is rejected. 
ACCEPTED REJECTED 
Yesterday's high and low are marked. Today's gap-up 
open sits inside that range. 
Result: continue with the remaining RF checks. 
Today's gap-up open exceeds yesterday's trading 
range, landing beyond yesterday's high. 
Result: the setup is not activated, even if everything 
else looks right. 
How to check it 
■ Mark yesterday's high and low as two horizontal lines on the chart. 
■ Look at where today's gap-up opens relative to those two lines. 
■ Inside the range: carry on with the RF checks. Beyond the range: no RF activation. 
 
IMPORTANT FILTER 
This works alongside the trend filter. An established uptrend and a gap up are still not enough: the size 
of the gap must stay within yesterday's trading range.

7  Stop-Loss Rule 
35-40 
points of stop-loss 
Pick 35 or 40 depending on what is logical for the particular setup. 
 
■ The stop-loss must be selected based on the trade structure and price movement. 
■ The choice between 35 and 40 points should be consistent with the trading rules being followed. 
■ Once the trade is entered, the stop-loss must be respected. 
Rule readiness 
Some parts of the strategy are fully specified; others are still open. Treat the open items as blanks to fill in before 
trading it.

C O R E  P R I N C I P L E  
RF is not a general breakout strategy. Its validity depends on the combination of an 
established uptrend, a gap-up opening within yesterday's trading range, a confirmed close 
below the first 5-minute candle's lower wick, and a subsequent return inside that range. 
A gap down, a gap beyond yesterday's range, a sudden gap-up reversal from a downtrend, or the 
absence of the required price sequence invalidates the setup.
