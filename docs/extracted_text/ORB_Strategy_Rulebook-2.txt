1. Strategy Overview 
The Opening Range Breakout (ORB) is an intraday method that uses the opening price action to identify a 
potential breakout (or breakdown). A trade is considered only when the trend, the level, and the quality of the 
first 5-minute candle all agree.

Core principle 
The ORB strategy is about identifying a confirmed breakout at the open — not buying or selling simply because 
price is moving. The correct level, the first candle's closing price, the 200 DMA filter (where applicable) and the 
absence of strong rejection wicks decide whether a setup qualifies. 
2. The First 5-Minute Candle 
The Indian market opens at 9:15 AM, so the first 5-minute candle covers 9:15 to 9:20 AM. Its close is not known 
until 9:20 — evaluate it only after it has completed. 
 
▶ Animation · The candle forms from 9:15 to 9:20; the checks are run at the close 
At 9:20 AM: 
1. Identify the previous day's resistance / support. 
2. Check the overall trend and the position of the 200 DMA / EMA on your chart. 
3. Evaluate the candle's body, upper wick and lower wick.

4. Confirm whether price has broken (and closed beyond) the relevant level. 
5. If the setup qualifies, take the trade by the rules below. 
 
Important 
The candle’s final shape and closing price are unknown before 9:20 AM. Never pre-empt the close. 
4. Method 1 — Previous-Day Level Breakout 
The primary ORB setup. It is used when the market is already trending and the first 5-minute candle breaks the 
previous day's key level in the direction of the trend. 
Long: previous-day resistance breakout 
 
▶ Animation · Uptrend, resistance breakout at 9:20, target hit 
1. Confirm the uptrend. Price above the 200 EMA is a basic confirmation, supported by bullish price 
structure. 
2. Mark previous-day resistance with a horizontal line. 
3. Check the 9:20 candle. It must break above and close above the level, with a strong green body and a 
small upper wick. 
4. Enter long at 9:20 AM if trend, breakout and candle quality are all satisfied. 
5. Apply fixed parameters: stop-loss 30, target 60 (1 : 2).

Short: previous-day support breakdown 
 
▶ Animation · Downtrend, support breakdown at 9:20, target hit 
1. Confirm the downtrend. Price below the 200 EMA, supported by bearish price structure. 
2. Mark previous-day support with a horizontal line. 
3. Check the 9:20 candle. It must break below and close below the level, with a strong red body and a small 
lower wick. 
4. Enter short at 9:20 AM if all conditions are satisfied. 
5. Apply fixed parameters: stop-loss 30, target 60 (1 : 2). 
5. Method 2 — Trendline & Inner Resistance / Support Breakout 
Method 2 is used when the market is trending but price has not broken the major previous-day level. That major 
resistance sits outside — at the top of the previous day’s range. Inside the previous day’s range there is usually a 
smaller resistance (a minor swing high or consolidation top). The break of that inner resistance — in an uptrend, 
near the rising trendline — is a valid ORB entry at 9:20 AM, even before the major resistance is broken.

Long: inner resistance breakout 
 
▶ Animation · Major resistance outside · inner resistance inside the previous day's range · trendline support 
1. Confirm the uptrend — price above the 200 EMA with bullish structure. 
2. Draw the rising trendline through the relevant swing lows. 
3. Mark the major previous-day resistance (outside) for context. 
4. Mark the inner resistance — the smaller level inside the previous day’s range. 
5. At 9:20 AM, check that the first candle breaks and closes above the inner resistance. 
6. Check candle strength — convincing green body, limited upper wick. 
7. Apply the 200 EMA filter — if the breakout is near the EMA, wait for a convincing close above it. 
8. Enter long with stop-loss 30 and target 60.

6. Special Setup — Gap Above / Below the 200 DMA 
A separate qualifying setup. Unlike Methods 1 and 2, it can be considered even when the market was previously 
trending the other way — provided the gap pushes price through the 200 DMA and the first 5-minute candle 
closes beyond it. 


--- PAGE 8 ---
 
 
Long: gap up above the 200 DMA 
 
▶ Animation · Gap up through the 200 DMA; candle closes above it 
1. Identify the gap up — the market opens above the previous close. 
2. Check the 200 DMA — price may have been below it; the gap must bring price above. 
3. Wait for the 9:20 close — a candle that only crosses the line but closes below it does not qualify. 
4. Confirm candle quality — a strong green body, or a suitable bullish pin-bar-type candle. 
5. Enter long with SL 30 and target 60. 


--- PAGE 9 ---
 
 
Short: gap down below the 200 DMA 
 
▶ Animation · Gap down through the 200 DMA; candle closes below it 
Apply the rules in reverse: the market gaps down below the previous close, the gap takes price below the 200 
DMA, and the first 5-minute candle closes below it with a strong red body (or a bearish pin-bar-type candle 
showing rejection of higher prices). Reject candles with a very large lower wick. 
Not every gap is valid 
This setup is an exception to the established-trend requirement — it does not mean every gap through the 200 
DMA qualifies. If the opening move is excessively extended, or the candle shows strong rejection wicks, reject 
the setup.

Standardize your units 
Decide and write down whether 30 and 60 mean index points, instrument price units, or rupee P&L. Price-
