02   Strategy Overview 
PDS stands for Previous Day Support. A support zone is identified from the previous trading day. The 
strategy then looks for a short-selling opportunity when that same zone acts as resistance on the 
following day. 
The key idea is a change of role: a level that held as support yesterday can act as resistance when the 
market opens gap down and price rises back to revisit it. PDS is not a breakdown strategy. The trader 
does not sell the moment price falls below yesterday’s support. The trader waits for price to climb back 
to the zone and for a confirmation to appear. 
 
Figure 1. The PDS setup from the previous day’s low to the short entry. 
Sequence: Previous day’s support, mark the zone, next-day gap-down open, price rises from below, 
support acts as resistance, entry confirmation (good red candle, red HPR pin bar, or small-support break), 
short trade.

03   Marking the Previous Day’s Support Zone 
Look at the previous day’s chart and find the low where price reached a level, found support, and moved 
upward. This is the area to mark and monitor in the next session. 
1 Identify the relevant low Find the low from the previous trading day where price took support. 
 
2 Locate the candle Identify the candle associated with that support area. 
 
3 Identify body and lower wick Both the relevant candle body and the lower wick belong to the zone. 
 
4 Draw a rectangle Draw a small rectangular zone covering the body and wick. Do not use a single horizontal line. 
 
5 Extend it forward Extend the rectangle horizontally so it stays visible on the next trading day. 
 
04   The Next-Day Gap-Down Condition 
The market must open gap down. After the open, price is trading below the previous day’s support zone. 
Do not enter just because the market opened gap down, or because price is trading below the previous 
support. The setup needs price to return upward and test the marked zone first. 
05   Previous Support Acting as Resistance 
This is the most important concept in PDS. On the previous day the zone acted as support: price reached 
it and moved up. On the next day the market opens below the zone. When price rises back to the same 
area, the zone is watched to see whether it now acts as resistance.

06   Price Reaching the Resistance Zone 
After the gap-down open, price must move back up toward the marked zone. The interaction must be 
close enough to count as a test of the zone, but it does not have to occur at one exact price. 
Valid interaction Meaning 
Wick touches the zone The candle’s wick reaches the marked area 
Body touches the zone The candle’s body reaches the marked area 
Price enters the zone Price moves inside the rectangle 
Price comes very close Close enough to qualify as a test of the zone 
07   Entry Conditions 
Once price has risen from below and tested the zone as resistance, a short is taken only when the 
rejection is of good quality. Methods 1 and 2 apply to the first red candle at the zone. If that red candle is 
not satisfying, use Method 3. All three methods target 70 points.

1 
Method 1: Good red candle 
The candle at the zone is a red candle with a good body: a clear, decisive rejection. Take the short on 
this first red candle only if it is a good candle. 
 
2 
Method 2: Red pin bar with higher price rejection (HPR) 
A pin bar showing higher price rejection: a long upper wick pushes into or above the zone and price is 
rejected. The candle must form red for the short to be activated. 
 
3 
Method 3: Break of a small support 
Use this when the first red candle is not satisfying (for example a weak or small-bodied candle). Wait 
for price to build a small support near the zone, then take the short when that support breaks. 
 
Quality rule: do not take a weak red candle. The first red candle must be a good candle (good body, or a 
red pin bar with higher price rejection). If it is not, wait for a small support to form and break (Method 3). 
Target and Stop-Loss 
The target is fixed at 70 points whichever entry condition occurs. The stop-loss is 35 or 40 points, chosen 
by the distance of the recent opposite candle. For this short setup, the opposite candle is the recent 
green candle.

Final Summary 
PDS is a short-selling strategy built on the previous day’s support. The low is marked as a rectangle 
covering the candle body and lower wick. On the next day the market must open gap down. Price then 
rises from below and returns to the zone, where the old support acts as resistance. The short is taken on 
a good red candle (Method 1) or a red pin bar showing higher price rejection (Method 2). If the red 
candle is not satisfying, wait for a small support to form and break (Method 3). The target is fixed at 70 
points, and the stop-loss is 35 or 40 points depending on the recent green candle’s distance.
