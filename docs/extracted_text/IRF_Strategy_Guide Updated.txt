IRF Strategy 
Initial Range Failure / Inverse Retest Failure 
Summary 
The Initial Range Failure (IRF) strategy is a rules-based, price-action framework for intraday short trades. 
It uses the first five-minute candle of the NSE session (9:15–9:20 AM) to define an initial range, then waits 
for a specific kind of failure: price pushes above the initial range high but cannot sustain the move. When 
that failure is confirmed by one of three defined entry methods, a short position is taken with a fixed ₹60 
target and a ₹35 or ₹40 stop-loss. 
IRF does not try to predict reversals. It waits for price to prove that an upward move has failed — and 
each of the three methods defines exactly what that proof looks like. This makes the strategy objective, 
repeatable and measurable.

Key Terms and Definitions 
The following terms are used throughout this guide. Precise definitions matter: most execution errors 
come from applying a term loosely. 
Term Definition 
Initial Range (IR) The full price range of the first five-minute candle of the session (9:15–9:20 AM), from its highest to its lowest traded price. 
Initial Range High (IRH) The highest price of the first candle, including its upper wick. The primary reference level for all three methods. 
Initial Range Low (IRL) The lowest price of the first candle, including its lower wick. 

--- PAGE 3 ---
 
 
Term Definition 
Close above IRH A five-minute candle whose closing price is above IRH. A wick above IRH with a close below it is not a close above IRH. 
Close back inside the range A candle whose closing price is back below IRH, within the initial range. 
Red candle A bearish candle: the close is below the open. 
Green candle A bullish candle: the close is above the open. 
Upper wick The part of a candle between the top of its body and its high. 
Recent support A price level formed by recent candles where price has held or turned upward. 
Recent resistance A recent high where price has stalled or turned downward — for example, the top of the first push after the breakout. 
Liquidity sweep A brief push above an obvious level — here, IRH — that triggers orders resting there and is then rejected, leaving a long upper wick. 
Opposite candle For a short trade, the most recent green candle. It is the reference used to select the stop-loss. 
Entry trigger The exact event that permits entry for a given method. 
 
Marking the Initial Range 
Every IRF trade begins with the initial range. Mark it carefully and in the same way every day — all three 
methods are measured against it. 
Step-by-Step Procedure 
1. Open the five-minute chart at the start of the session. 
2. Identify the first candle — the candle covering 9:15 AM to 9:20 AM. 
3. Wait for it to close. The range is final only once this candle has closed at 9:20 AM. 
4. Mark the high. Draw a horizontal line at the highest price of the candle, including its upper wick. 
This is the Initial Range High (IRH). 
5. Mark the low. Draw a horizontal line at the lowest price of the candle, including its lower wick. This 
is the Initial Range Low (IRL). 
6. Monitor price around IRH. All three methods focus on price moving above IRH and then showing 
signs of failure or reversal.

Method 1 — Initial Range Breakout and Failure 
Concept 
Price breaks above the initial range high and the breakout candle closes above it. The very next candle, 
however, fails to sustain the breakout and closes back inside the initial range. That failure is the short-
entry signal. 
 
Figure 2 — Method 1 — breakout candle closes above IRH; the next candle closes back inside the range 
Entry Rules 
Step Condition Requirement 
1 Initial range marked IRH and IRL drawn from the 9:15–9:20 AM candle, wicks included 
2 Breakout Price breaks above IRH 
3 Breakout close The breakout candle closes above IRH 
4 Failure The next candle closes back inside the initial range 
5 Entry Short immediately at the close of that candle 
 
E N T R Y  T R I G G E R 
Enter short at the close of the candle that closes back inside the range — the candle immediately after 
the breakout candle. No support or resistance break is required. 
 


--- PAGE 6 ---
 
 
What Does Not Qualify 
• A wick above IRH without a close above it. There is no breakout candle, so Method 1 has not 
started. 
• A next candle that dips inside the range but closes above IRH. A wick back into the range does not 
confirm the failure — the close must be inside. 
• Entering before the next candle closes. The trigger is the close, not the move. 
• Waiting for a support or resistance break. Method 1 does not require one. Waiting moves the 
entry away from the defined trigger. 
Worked Example 
Suppose the initial range high is 100. 
Candle Action 
Candle 1 Breaks above 100 and closes at 103 — a valid breakout close above IRH. 
Candle 2 Closes at 99 — back inside the initial range. 
Result The short-entry signal activates at the close of Candle 2, at 99. 
Prices are illustrative only. Every trade follows the defined entry and risk-management rules. 
 
K E Y  R U L E 
The second candle must close back inside the initial range. A wick moving back into the range without the 
required close does not satisfy the entry condition.

Method 2 — Extended Upward Move and Reversal 
Concept 
Method 2 looks for an upward extension beyond the initial range, followed by a reversal signal. Unlike 
Method 1, price does not break out and immediately close back inside the range. Instead, price closes 
above the initial range high, continues upward, forms a red candle, and then breaks a relevant recent 
level. 
That level can be recent support or recent resistance — both are valid confirmations, and both are 
explained in full below. 
Entry Rules 
Step Condition Requirement 
1 Initial range marked IRH and IRL drawn from the 9:15–9:20 AM candle 
2 Breakout close Price breaks above IRH and closes above it 
3 Extension Price extends further upward beyond the initial range 
4 Red candle A red candle forms during the upward extension 
5 Reference level The relevant recent level is identified — recent support or recent resistance 
6 Level break Support: a candle closes below it. Resistance: a red candle closes back below it. The short is taken on this break. 
 
E N T R Y  T R I G G E R 
Take the short when the relevant recent level breaks — either a candle closes below recent support, or a 
red candle closes back below recent resistance after price has traded above it.

Confirmation A — Support Break 
Price extends above the initial range and leaves a recent support level behind it. Once a red candle 
appears, the short is confirmed when a candle closes below that support. 
1. A candle closes above IRH. 
2. Price extends further upward; a pullback low forms recent support. 
3. A red candle forms. 
4. A candle closes below recent support — enter short at that close.

Confirmation B — Resistance Break 
Here the extension stalls at a recent high, which becomes resistance. Price later trades back above that 
level but cannot hold there. When a red candle closes back below the resistance, the attempt to extend 
has failed — buyers who bought above that level are now trapped. 
1. A candle closes above IRH. 
2. The first push tops out — that high is the recent resistance. 
3. Price pulls back, then pushes back above the resistance. 
4. A red candle forms. 
5. A red candle closes back below the resistance — enter short at that close. 
 
Figure 4 — Method 2, resistance break — price pushes back above recent resistance, then a red candle closes back below it 
R E C O M M E N D E D  P R A C T I C E 
Confirm either break on a candle close, not an intra-candle wick: a close below support, or a close back 
below resistance. This keeps Method 2 objective and consistent with the close-based triggers of Methods 
1 and 3. 
 
What Does Not Qualify 
• A red candle with no level break. A red candle by itself is not sufficient confirmation. 
• Price still holding above resistance. Until a red candle closes back below the resistance, the push 
above it has not failed. 
• No close above IRH. If price only wicked above the range, Method 2 has not started. 
• An immediate close back inside the range. If the candle after the breakout closes back inside, the 
setup is Method 1, not Method 2. 
• Insisting on a support break only. Method 2 accepts a relevant support break or a relevant 
resistance break. The compulsory support-break rule belongs to Method 3.

Method 3 — Advanced IRF / Liquidity Sweep 
Concept 
Method 3 is the advanced IRF setup, built around a liquidity sweep above the initial range high. Price first 
forms small candles inside the initial range. A subsequent red candle then develops a significant upper 
wick that extends beyond IRH. The critical confirmation is a break of recent support. 
The recent support break is compulsory. A wick extending above the initial range high is not, on its own, 
enough to qualify as this setup. 
 
Figure 5 — Method 3 — small candles inside the range, a red candle sweeps above IRH and closes below recent support 
Entry Rules 
Step Condition Requirement 
1 Initial range marked IRH and IRL drawn from the 9:15–9:20 AM candle 
2 Compression Small candles form inside the initial range 
3 Sweep candle A red candle forms with a significant upper wick extending beyond IRH 
4 Wick criterion Approximately 60–70% of the upper wick is outside (above) IRH 
5 Reference level The recent support formed by the preceding candles is identified 
6 Support break The red candle closes below that recent support — compulsory 
 


--- PAGE 12 ---
 
 
E N T R Y  T R I G G E R 
The setup is valid only when the red sweep candle closes below recent support. That support break is the 
entry confirmation. 
 
The Wick Condition 
The upper wick should extend significantly above the initial range high. The guide is that approximately 
60–70% of the upper wick lies outside the range. It is measured as follows: 
Measure Calculation 
Upper wick Candle high − top of the body (the open, for a red candle) 
Portion above IRH Candle high − IRH 
Share outside the range Portion above IRH ÷ upper wick 
 
Illustration. IRH is 100. A red candle opens at 92, reaches a high of 115 and closes at 82. The upper wick is 
115 − 92 = 23. The portion above IRH is 115 − 100 = 15. The share outside the range is 15 ÷ 23 ≈ 65% — 
within the 60–70% guide. Recent support is at 85, and the candle closes at 82, below it: the support break 
is confirmed. 
The percentage is an approximate qualifying feature. It is never a substitute for the support-break confirmation. 
The Most Important Rule: The Support Break 
K E Y  R U L E  —  T H E  S U P P O R T  B R E A K  I S  C O M P U L S O R Y 
• Recent support must be identified. 
• Price must close below that support. 
• If the wick sweeps above the initial range but recent support does not break, the Method 3 
confirmation is absent — there is no trade. 
Method 3 is not confirmed by the liquidity sweep alone. The recent support break is the essential 
condition. 
 
What Does Not Qualify 
• A sweep candle that closes above recent support. The rejection is visible, but sellers have not 
followed through. 
• A wick that barely clears IRH. If only a small part of the wick is above the range, the significant 
sweep the method describes has not occurred. 
• A large, trending advance instead of small candles inside the range. Method 3 begins with 
compression inside the range.

Telling the Methods Apart in Real Time 
• A candle has closed above IRH. Watch the very next candle. If it closes back inside the range, the 
setup is Method 1 — enter at that close. 
• Price holds above IRH and keeps rising. The setup can only become Method 2. Wait for a red 
candle, then a close below recent support or a red candle closing back below recent resistance. 
• Price stays inside the range with small candles, then a red candle spikes above IRH. Assess 
Method 3 — and act only if that candle closes below recent support. 
M E T H O D  2  V S  M E T H O D  3  —  T H E  C R I T I C A L  D I F F E R E N C E 
Method 2 accepts a relevant support or resistance break and needs a close above IRH first; Method 3 
involves a sweep (wick) above IRH, uses the ≈60–70% wick criterion, and requires a support break. 
Applying one method's confirmation to the other is one of the most common causes of poor execution.

G O L D E N  R U L E S 
• Mark IRH and IRL first — wicks included. 
• Closes confirm; wicks do not. 
• Apply one method's rules at a time. 
• Place the stop-loss and ₹60 target immediately, and never widen the stop. 
• Record every trade by method and review each method separately.
